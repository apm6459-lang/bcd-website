Your Next Steps

Every bankruptcy case is different, but these are some of the major steps that commonly occur after an individual files.

1

Watch for Your Bankruptcy Case Notice

The court sends a notice containing important information about your bankruptcy case. It may include your case number, the trustee assigned to the case, important deadlines and information about your meeting of creditors.

Timing and procedures can vary by bankruptcy court.
2

Understand the Automatic Stay

Filing bankruptcy generally creates an automatic stay that stops many collection actions. This may affect collection calls, lawsuits, garnishments and certain other efforts to collect debts.

Important exceptions and limitations can apply. The automatic stay does not stop every type of action in every case.
3

Work With Your Bankruptcy Trustee

A trustee is assigned to administer aspects of your bankruptcy case. The trustee may review your petition and financial information and request documents before your meeting of creditors.

4

Provide Required Documents

Individual debtors may be required to provide certain financial documents to the trustee. For example, federal bankruptcy information states that debtors must provide the trustee with a copy of the most recently filed federal income tax return or transcript before the meeting of creditors.

Follow instructions from your trustee, attorney and bankruptcy court regarding documents and deadlines.
5

Prepare for Your 341 Meeting

The meeting of creditors, often called the 341 meeting, is an important part of the bankruptcy process. The debtor answers questions under oath about financial affairs and information contained in the bankruptcy filings.

Learn more about the 341 meeting →
6

If You're in Chapter 13, Begin Following Plan Requirements

Chapter 13 works differently from Chapter 7. Under federal bankruptcy information, Chapter 13 debtors generally must begin making plan payments within 30 days after filing, even if the plan has not yet been confirmed.

Explore Chapter 13 →
7

Stay Informed Throughout Your Case

Keep track of court notices, deadlines, trustee requests and changes in your case. Your bankruptcy does not end when the petition is filed. Staying organized throughout the process is important.

Questions After Filing Bankruptcy

What happens immediately after I file?

Once a bankruptcy petition is filed, a bankruptcy case is opened. The court processes the case, a trustee is assigned, listed creditors receive notice of the bankruptcy, and important dates and deadlines are established. Filing also generally puts the automatic stay into effect.

What information is on my bankruptcy notice?

A bankruptcy case notice generally contains identifying information about the case along with important dates, deadlines, trustee information and details about the meeting of creditors. Read court notices carefully and keep copies with your bankruptcy records.

What does the trustee do?

The trustee's responsibilities depend on the chapter and circumstances of the case. Trustees review bankruptcy information, conduct the meeting of creditors and perform other duties required under bankruptcy law.

Do I still pay my bills after filing?

Filing bankruptcy does not mean every payment should automatically stop. Treatment of mortgages, vehicle loans, leases, utilities, secured debts and other obligations can differ. Follow your bankruptcy documents and seek qualified legal advice if you are unsure how a particular payment should be handled.

What happens to my car after filing?

What happens to a vehicle depends on factors such as the bankruptcy chapter, whether the vehicle is financed or leased, available exemptions, equity in the vehicle and how the debt is treated in the case.

What happens to my house?

Homeownership issues in bankruptcy can depend on equity, exemptions, mortgage status, the chapter filed and other circumstances. Bankruptcy can affect foreclosure activity through the automatic stay, but the stay is not necessarily permanent.

What happens next in Chapter 7?

Chapter 7 generally involves a trustee reviewing the case, a meeting of creditors and, in many individual cases, a potential discharge later in the process if legal requirements are satisfied and no applicable objection prevents discharge.

Visit the Chapter 7 Guide →

What happens next in Chapter 13?

Chapter 13 generally involves a repayment plan lasting three to five years. Debtors begin plan payments early in the case, attend the meeting of creditors and proceed through the plan-confirmation process.

Visit the Chapter 13 Guide →

What should I avoid doing after filing?

Do not ignore court notices, trustee requests or deadlines. Avoid making assumptions about how bankruptcy affects property, payments or debts. If you are represented by an attorney, contact your attorney when you are uncertain about an action that could affect your case.
