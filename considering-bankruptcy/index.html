What Is Bankruptcy?

Bankruptcy is a legal process handled in federal court. Bankruptcy laws provide a process for addressing certain debts while also providing rights and protections to creditors.

For individuals, Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 are two of the most common forms of bankruptcy. They work differently and may be appropriate in different circumstances.

Is Bankruptcy Right for Me?

There is no single answer that applies to everyone. Whether bankruptcy should be considered depends on factors such as your debts, income, expenses, property, financial goals, and other individual circumstances.

BCD can help you understand the bankruptcy process and organize questions you may want to ask, but deciding whether to file is an individual legal and financial decision.

What Is the Difference Between Chapter 7 and Chapter 13?

Chapter 7 generally does not involve creating a repayment plan. A trustee administers the case and may sell property that is not protected by applicable exemptions. Many individual Chapter 7 cases are no-asset cases.

Chapter 13 allows an individual with regular income to propose a plan to repay all or part of their debts over time. Chapter 13 plans generally run for three to five years.

Learn more through BCD's Chapter 7 and Chapter 13 resources.

What May Happen to My House, Car, or Other Property?

What happens to property during bankruptcy depends on several factors, including the bankruptcy chapter, applicable exemptions, liens, payment status, and the circumstances of the individual case.

Because property issues can have significant legal and financial consequences, questions about a particular home, vehicle, or other asset should be discussed with a qualified bankruptcy attorney.

What Types of Debt Can Bankruptcy Help With?

Bankruptcy may provide relief from many types of debt, but a bankruptcy discharge does not necessarily eliminate every debt.

Bankruptcy law identifies categories of debts that may not be dischargeable. The treatment of a particular debt can also depend on the bankruptcy chapter and circumstances of the case.

Are There Alternatives to Bankruptcy?

Bankruptcy is not the only option for dealing with financial problems. Depending on the situation, people may explore budgeting changes, negotiating with creditors, repayment arrangements, credit counseling, or other approaches.

The advantages, disadvantages, costs, and risks of different options can vary. Understanding your complete financial situation can help you identify which questions to explore before making a decision.

When Should I Speak With a Bankruptcy Attorney?

BCD provides educational information, but it cannot provide legal advice or determine whether bankruptcy is appropriate for your individual situation.

A qualified bankruptcy attorney can review your individual circumstances, explain how bankruptcy law may apply to you, and answer questions about your debts, property, eligibility, and other legal issues.

BCD is working to expand resources that can help consumers connect with qualified professionals in their area. Attorney availability through BCD may vary by location.
