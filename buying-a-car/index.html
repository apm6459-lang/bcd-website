Can I Buy a Car After Bankruptcy?

Bankruptcy does not automatically prevent you from purchasing or financing another vehicle. Consumers may have financing opportunities after bankruptcy, but approval and terms depend on the individual applicant and the lender's underwriting requirements.

Factors may include your current income, employment, debts, credit history, down payment, vehicle price, amount financed, and loan term.

After discharge, the best next step is often to understand your current financial position and compare realistic vehicle and financing options rather than assuming bankruptcy means you cannot purchase a vehicle.

Can I Buy a Car If I Just Filed Bankruptcy?

Filing bankruptcy does not necessarily mean vehicle financing is unavailable. There are dealerships and financing sources that work with consumers who have recently filed bankruptcy.

The options available to you can depend on your bankruptcy chapter, income, current debts, current vehicle situation, court requirements, lender requirements, and other individual factors.

BCD can help you understand your stage of bankruptcy and connect you with participating resources familiar with bankruptcy vehicle financing.

Can I Finance a Vehicle During Chapter 13?

Some Chapter 13 debtors may be able to purchase and finance a vehicle while their repayment plan is active.

The exact process varies. Depending on your court, trustee, bankruptcy plan, and circumstances, additional approval or documentation may be required before taking on new vehicle debt.

A bankruptcy-experienced dealership or financing source may be familiar with the vehicle-financing process, but questions about court or trustee requirements should be addressed with your bankruptcy attorney, trustee, or court as appropriate.

What Determines My Auto Loan Terms?

According to the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau, lenders may consider several factors when determining the interest rate and terms offered on an auto loan.

These factors can include your credit history and credit score, income, debts, loan amount, loan term, vehicle, and down payment.

Different lenders may evaluate the same applicant differently, which is one reason comparing available financing options can be valuable.

Do I Need a Down Payment After Bankruptcy?

Down payment requirements vary by lender, dealership, vehicle, and applicant.

A larger down payment can reduce the amount of money being financed and may affect the overall structure of the loan. However, there is no single down-payment amount that applies to everyone after bankruptcy.

Before purchasing, consider both the money required upfront and whether the resulting monthly payment fits comfortably into your budget.

Can Financing a Vehicle Help Rebuild My Credit?

A vehicle loan can create an opportunity to establish positive payment history if the lender reports your payments to credit reporting companies and you consistently make payments on time.

However, taking on a vehicle loan solely for the purpose of improving credit is not necessarily appropriate for everyone.

The vehicle and payment should make sense for your actual transportation needs and overall budget. Building credit works best when new debt is manageable and payments can be made consistently.
