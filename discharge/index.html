Discharge

A Fresh Start Through a Bankruptcy Discharge

A bankruptcy discharge releases you from personal liability for certain debts. This means you are no longer legally required to pay debts that have been discharged, and creditors are permanently prohibited from trying to collect those discharged debts.

Receiving a discharge is an important step in the bankruptcy process — and the beginning of your financial fresh start.

What Does a Bankruptcy Discharge Mean?

A bankruptcy discharge releases a debtor from personal liability for certain specified debts. Once a debt has been discharged, the debtor is no longer legally required to pay that debt.

The discharge also operates as a permanent order prohibiting creditors from taking collection action on discharged debts, including certain lawsuits, telephone calls, letters, and other collection communications.

When Does a Bankruptcy Discharge Occur?

The timing depends on the bankruptcy chapter and the circumstances of the case.

According to U.S. Courts, a Chapter 7 discharge is typically entered after the applicable objection periods have expired and commonly occurs about four months after the bankruptcy petition is filed.

In Chapter 13, discharge generally occurs after the debtor completes the payments required under the confirmed repayment plan and satisfies other applicable requirements.

Are All Debts Discharged?

No. The debts covered by a discharge depend on the bankruptcy chapter and the individual case.

Bankruptcy law identifies categories of debt that may not be dischargeable. Examples can include certain taxes, domestic support obligations, certain educational loans, fines and penalties, and other debts identified by bankruptcy law.

Questions about whether a particular debt was discharged should be discussed with a qualified bankruptcy attorney.

What If a Creditor Tries to Collect a Discharged Debt?

A bankruptcy discharge creates a permanent injunction against collection of debts covered by the discharge.

U.S. Courts explains that if a creditor attempts to collect a discharged debt, a debtor may be able to ask the bankruptcy court to address the violation. Creditors that violate the discharge injunction may face court sanctions.

What Happens to Secured Debts and Liens?

A discharge can eliminate personal liability for a debt without necessarily eliminating a valid lien attached to property.

If a valid lien was not avoided or otherwise made unenforceable during the bankruptcy case, the secured creditor may still have rights concerning the property securing that debt.

How Can I Get Another Copy of My Discharge Order?

If a discharge order is lost or misplaced, another copy may be available from the clerk of the bankruptcy court that entered the order.

Bankruptcy records may also be available electronically through the federal PACER system. Court and PACER fees may apply.
