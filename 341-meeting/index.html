MEETING OF CREDITORS

Your 341 Meeting:
What to Expect

The 341 Meeting of Creditors is a required part of the bankruptcy process. It gives the trustee an opportunity to review your case and ask questions about the information you provided in your bankruptcy documents.

What is a 341 Meeting?

It is a meeting conducted by your bankruptcy trustee. You answer questions under oath about your bankruptcy case, finances, property, debts, and the information contained in your petition and schedules.

No Judge The bankruptcy judge does not attend.
Usually Brief Many meetings last about 10–15 minutes.
Under Oath You must answer the trustee truthfully.

341 Meeting Preparation Checklist

Your trustee or local court may have additional requirements, so always follow the instructions provided for your specific case.

✓

Government-Issued Photo ID

Have an acceptable photo identification document available, such as a driver's license, state-issued ID, or passport.

✓

Proof of Social Security Number

Be prepared to provide acceptable verification of your Social Security number as required by the trustee.

✓

Most Recently Filed Tax Return

Individual debtors are generally required to provide their most recently filed federal income tax return or transcript to the trustee before the meeting.

✓

Review Your Petition & Schedules

Review the documents filed in your case so you are familiar with the information the trustee may ask you about.

✓

Financial Records Requested by the Trustee

Have any bank statements, investment statements, pay information, or additional records your trustee specifically requested.

✓

Your Meeting Instructions

Confirm your meeting date, time, trustee information, and instructions for attending or joining the meeting.

✓

Be Ready to Answer Questions Under Oath

The trustee may ask about your income, property, debts, financial history, and information contained in your bankruptcy documents.

YOUR MEETING

What Happens at a 341 Meeting?

1

Your Identity Is Verified

The trustee confirms your identity using the identification and Social Security verification required for the meeting.

2

You Are Placed Under Oath

You must swear or affirm that the answers you provide are truthful.

3

The Trustee Reviews Your Case

The trustee may ask about your petition, schedules, income, debts, property, transfers, or other matters affecting your bankruptcy.

4

Creditors May Ask Questions

Creditors receive notice and may attend and ask relevant questions, although they are not required to attend.

5

The Meeting Is Concluded or Continued

Many meetings are completed quickly. The trustee may continue the meeting if additional information or documentation is needed.

6

Your Bankruptcy Continues

After the meeting, your case moves into its next stage based on whether you filed Chapter 7, Chapter 13, or another bankruptcy chapter.

QUESTIONS & ANSWERS

Understanding Your 341 Meeting

When does the 341 meeting happen?

The timing depends on the bankruptcy chapter. Official bankruptcy court guidance explains that meetings generally occur between 21 and 50 days after the bankruptcy petition is filed.

Will I have to appear before a bankruptcy judge?

No. The meeting of creditors is conducted outside the presence of the bankruptcy judge. In Chapter 7, Chapter 12, and Chapter 13 cases, the trustee assigned to the case generally conducts the meeting.

What does the trustee ask about?

The trustee may review your petition and schedules and ask about your property, debts, income, financial condition, prior transactions, and other information that may affect the administration of your bankruptcy case.

Will creditors attend?

Creditors receive notice of the meeting and are permitted to attend and ask questions related to the bankruptcy case. They are not required to attend.

How long does a 341 meeting take?

The U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the Northern District of California states that the meeting usually lasts about 10 to 15 minutes, although a meeting may be continued if additional information is needed.

What if my spouse and I filed together?

If spouses filed a joint bankruptcy case, both debtors must participate in the meeting of creditors.

What happens if I do not attend?

Attendance is required. Failure to appear or provide requested information can put the case at risk, including possible dismissal or other relief requested by the trustee.

Can the meeting be continued?

Yes. If the trustee needs additional documents, information, or answers, the meeting may be continued to another date.
