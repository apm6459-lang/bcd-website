Chapter 7 is a type of bankruptcy commonly referred to as “liquidation.” Unlike Chapter 13, it does not involve creating a repayment plan. A bankruptcy trustee may gather and sell property that is not protected by applicable exemptions and use the proceeds to pay creditors. Many Chapter 7 cases involving individuals, however, are “no asset” cases in which there is no distribution to unsecured creditors. For individuals who qualify, one of the primary purposes of Chapter 7 is to discharge certain debts and provide a financial fresh start. A discharge can release an individual from personal liability for many debts, although some debts are not dischargeable and individual circumstances vary.
